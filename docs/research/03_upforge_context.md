### 3.2 Where a boutique can win in 2026 [ASSUMPTION, grounded in the benchmarks above]
1. **Speed.** "Answer in 4 weeks, not 12." The site already claims this with "Shipped by Week 4".
2. **AI-augmented research leverage.** 53% of researchers now use AI regularly, and teams using synthetic data, agentic AI or purpose-built tools are far more likely to report growing demand (72%). [FOUND-WEB, Qualtrics 2026 Market Research Trends] A boutique that runs desk research, competitor teardowns, review/social mining and model scaffolding through AI agents can deliver MBB-shaped output at 1/20th the price. Shardul's own *Launch Intelligence* dossier (130 pages studied by research agents) proves the workflow.
3. **Price arbitrage from an India cost base**, sold in USD to US/UK/GCC buyers.
4. **Founder-led senior attention.** The founder, not a second-year analyst, is on every call.
5. **Corridor specialisation** in India↔UK, India↔UAE/GCC, US↔India and (from 2027) EU↔India, where trade agreements are creating new entrants every month.
6. **Consumer psychology as the differentiator.** Most market-entry shops are supply-side (entity, tax, distribution). Few answer "why will this consumer trust, try and pay?" The website's hero line and the Finnish-jewellery and Dubai Gen Z testimonials already sit here.
7. **Strategy + model + execution in one engagement** (the EaaS DNA). Big 4 and research houses stop at the report.

### 3.3 Threats and weaknesses [ASSUMPTION]
- Credibility gap: no named case studies, a young LinkedIn presence, and a team without titles.
- AI scepticism: buyers worry about hallucinated numbers. This must be met with a published sourcing and verification method.
- Self-serve AI tools: executives can now run their own "deep research". Upforge's value must be judgement, primary research (interviews, surveys) and the model, not compilation.
- Generalist drift: the directory lists web dev and HR, which dilutes the specialist premium.

### 5.1 How AI-augmented research makes the margins work [ASSUMPTION; illustrative unit economics]
- **Where the AI does the work:**
  - Desk research and source gathering (research agents crawl regulators, trade data, competitor sites, marketplaces and reviews, as in the Launch Intelligence dossier).
  - Competitor teardowns and price scraping.
  - Review and social-listening synthesis.
  - Interview guide drafting and transcript coding.
  - Survey design and open-end coding.
  - Model scaffolding and scenario documentation.
  - First-draft slides and memos.
- **Where humans do the work:** expert and consumer interviews, judgement calls, the "so what", verification of every number against a primary source, client workshops, and the final model logic.
- **Illustrative Sprint at $12,000:**
  - ~25 hours of founder/senior time, ~60 hours of analyst time, ~$300–$800 of tools/data, plus 6–8 expert/consumer calls (~$600–$1,500 in incentives).
  - At India-based loaded costs (e.g., ~$60–$100/hr senior opportunity cost, ~$15–$30/hr analyst), delivery cost is ~$3,500–$6,000 → **~50–70% gross margin**.
  - Without AI, the same scope would need ~150–200 analyst hours.
- **Rules that protect quality:**
  - A sourcing ledger for every number (primary source, date, confidence).
  - A "known vs estimated vs undisclosed" convention. The firm's AI-alliance insights pages already do this, so make it a signature.
  - Human sign-off before delivery.

### 5.2 Pricing tactics [ASSUMPTION]
- Publish "from" prices for tiers 1–3 on the website. This is a rare move among consultancies, it pre-qualifies leads, and it lets the app quote in emails.
- Credit the Pulse Brief and Audit toward the Sprint (land-and-expand).
- Fixed fee, not day rates. Optionally add a small success kicker on retainers (e.g., a bonus on first-90-day revenue milestones). This matches the industry shift toward outcome-based fees.
- Use 3 price points per proposal (Good / Better / Best).
- India pricing: quote in INR plus GST for India-HQ buyers. Quote in USD for India-HQ buyers expanding abroad when the budget sits in a foreign entity.
