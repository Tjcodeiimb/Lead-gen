# Voice (from the Transline content pack and the website; docs/research/03 §1.4)

- Open on a concrete, checkable observation about THEM, never on us.
- Specific, verifiable numbers only. Specificity is the single biggest difference between approved and rejected drafts.
- Operator-to-operator register: capital already spent, pilot vs running, shelf space, landed cost.
- One quotable line per piece at most.
- Flag any unsourced claim explicitly rather than soften it into vagueness.
- Plain English. No hype, no flattery, no exclamation marks, no emojis.
- Do NOT use the website's "we hate traditional consulting" or "stop wasting money" lines in cold outreach. They read as aggressive to a CFO.
- Email first touch: under 120 words, one ask (a 20-minute call OR the free 2-page pulse), one signal, one insight.
- LinkedIn note: under 300 characters, a reason to connect grounded in one fact, no pitch, no price.
- Always end an email with the opt-out line from compliance.md.

## Cold email craft (adapted from the public principles of the Cold Email Hall of Fame, coldemails.world)

The first email has at most FIVE sentences, in this order. Fewer is better.
1. **Why them, why now.** One specific, checkable thing they did recently (a launch, a hire, a filing). Never "I came across your profile".
2. **Who I am, in one line.** Only what makes the next sentence credible. No company history.
3. **Proof of work.** Something already done for them: one finding, one number, one pattern from their market. Give before you ask.
4. **One ask, easy to say yes to.** Answerable with yes or no. A specific small thing: "Want the two-page version?" or "Worth 15 minutes on Thursday?" Never two asks.
5. **Sign-off** (name, title, one line), then the opt-out line. These do not count toward the five.

Voice and rhythm:
- Write the way you would type to a respected peer. Contractions are fine. Read it aloud; if you would not say it, cut it.
- Vary sentence length. One short sentence lands harder after a longer one.
- No preamble ("I hope", "My name is", "I wanted to reach out"). Start on them.
- If there is no common ground, the common ground is their public move. Make the first sentence about it.

Subject lines:
- Two to five words, lowercase, about THEM or the specific thing: "noon uae launch", "your country manager hire".
- Should read like an internal note from a colleague, not a newsletter. No colons, no questions, no "quick".

Follow-ups (polite persistence):
- Each follow-up adds one new thing: a second data point, a relevant example, or a smaller ask. Never "just bumping this".
- Two to three sentences, written as a reply in the same thread.
- Maximum three follow-ups, then a gracious close that leaves the door open.
